# Trackside Pass

A race-day companion web app (PWA): track maps and walking directions, schedules, weather, and a Race Day hub. Plain HTML/CSS/JS, no framework. The user is a fan who uses it in person at race weekends, mostly on a phone, so it has to work on a phone with poor signal.

## Layout
- `index.html`, `css/styles.css`, `js/app.js` (the app; section index at its top), `js/passport.js`
- `data/<track-id>.js` one file per track (POIs, sessions, events, facts, outline: small, loaded at startup) plus
  `data/maps/<track-id>.js` (base map, walking grid, elevation: ~90% of the bytes, fetched when the track is opened by
  `ensureMap()` in `js/app.js`; `openTrack()` returns a Promise). **Edit events/POIs in `data/<id>.js` as before.** Tools that
  rewrite a whole track go through `build_route.load()/save()`, which merge and re-split automatically. After the Ruby
  builder (`build_permanent.rb`) writes a whole file, run `python3 tools/track-data/split_tracks.py <id>`.
  Never add `base`/`route`/`elev` back into the light file. `data/series.js` series guides; `data/watch.js` "What to watch"; `data/results.js` finished-race results; `data/champs.js` standings
- `sw.js` service worker. Its `VERSION` is set to the commit hash by `build.sh` at deploy time, so phones refresh on every deploy and nobody bumps it by hand (the `vNNN` in the source is ignored).
- `build.sh` copies the live files into `dist/` and stamps the service-worker version; Netlify runs it on every push to `main`. It first runs `node tools/validate.js` (syntax, data loads, cross-references, sw/index/data consistency): if that fails the build fails and Netlify keeps serving the last good version. `.github/workflows/validate.yml` runs the same check on every push. Adding a track file to `data/` is all that is needed for the track lists: `node tools/sync_tracks.js` (run by build.sh) makes `index.html` and `sw.js` match the folder. Run `node tools/validate.js` before pushing data changes; `SKIP_VALIDATE=1` skips it in an emergency.

## Accuracy rule
Never invent facts. Race facts (results, standings, points, cars, drivers) must come from published sources (imsa.com, racer.com, sportscar365.com, series sites, Porsche, etc.). Cross-check numbers. Do not use Wikipedia for numbers (its standings were wrong) -- except circuit lap records, where Wikipedia is fine (the user OK'd it). If something cannot be verified, leave it out and say so in the entry's `note`. Do not use IMSA/Alkamel live timing data: it carries an explicit no-redistribution notice. The same goes for Porsche's live timing pages (racing.porsche.com/.../live-timing, Carrera Cup and Supercup): at Formula 1 weekends they carry "© Timing Data Formula One World Championship Limited. All rights reserved" (checked Oct 24 2026), so link out to them (the hub's Live timing card does) but never read results from them; Carrera Cup results come from published race reports (Porsche newsroom, IMSA, sportscar365).

## Track data: walking routes, crossings, elevation (`tools/track-data/`)
Every track has a walking grid (`route`). Three things make Directions actually work -- check all three for a new track:
- **Crossings.** A walking route crosses the circuit ONLY at badges of kind `cross`, and each must sit within 60 m of the
  circuit `path` or the app can't link it (a mis-placed crossing silently strands the infield -- Daytona's were 79 and 161 m
  off). `python3 tools/track-data/find_cross.py <id>` lists real bridges/tunnels over the circuit from OpenStreetMap;
  `add_cross.py <id> --write` adds the vetted ones (footway/path, named, or explicitly permissive; never `access=private`).
- **Walking grid.** `build_route.py <id>` regenerates `route` from the track's own `base` map data. Only use it for tracks
  with no grid; don't overwrite a hand-tuned one.
- **Elevation** (hill notes in Directions). `elev_build.py <id>` downloads a ~100 m grid (USGS in the US, Open-Meteo for
  Canada), `apply_elev.py <id>` bakes it in. Only worth it where the terrain has relief; flat tracks (Daytona, Sebring,
  Indianapolis road course and oval) are skipped on purpose.
- **Check:** in the browser console, from a gate / restroom / food / parking badge, `walkMetresTo()` should reach >= 98% of a
  track's badges. Don't bump a track's `rev` for this: it wipes the user's own badge edits.

## Working on js/app.js
- Section index is at the top of the file. Every localStorage key lives in the `K` table near the top (add new keys there; `tools/validate.js` fails on stray `paddock:` literals). A series page (`renderSeries`) lists every upcoming race via `allOrderOf(c)`, so a venue that hosts the series twice (HSR at Daytona, F1 at COTA in 2026 and 2027) shows once per weekend; `orderOf(c)` (one next race per venue) is for the home tile and the soonest-race logic. The Race Day hub is one builder function per card (`hubSessionCard`, `hubWatchCard`, ...) composed by `renderHub()`.
- Before refactoring anything the hub renders, run the regression harness in `tools/hub_snapshot.js` (instructions in its header): it hashes the hub HTML for every track at ~10 pretend moments, so "nothing changed" can be proven.

## Home and picks behaviour (Oct 31 2026)
The big Home tile (`renderHero`, `heroPick()`) is the weekend you marked "I'm going" if it is within `goDays()`, else the soonest-starting weekend of a series you follow (`anyOrder()`; weekends starting the same day go to the series you arranged first in Settings). "Your series, next up" (headed "This week" for weekends starting by Sunday, "Coming up" for the rest of the 14-day window) skips the weekend the big tile shows, and a card opens that track's Race Day hub (`openTrack`; only a "Map coming soon" stop opens the series schedule). A weekend of a series without championship pages (HSR) shows no other series' standings or What to watch (`vintage` in `hubFocusSeries`). Pick your podium: tap a filled slot, then another slot, to swap or move a pick (`gameSlot`, `gameSel`; the x on a slot removes it); an entry may carry `lockRace` (a regex on the race session name) so picks lock at that race instead of the weekend's first race (MotoGP: `^Race - MotoGP`, the Sunday Grand Prix, not the Saturday Sprint); `tools/sync_games.mjs` reads the same field.

## Race Day hub is the landing page
Opening any track lands on the Hub tab (`enterHub()` in `openTrackNow`); Map is the second tab, then Events and Info. The back arrow and swipe-back step Map/Events/Info -> Hub -> track list (`goHome`). Off-season the hub's top card shows the next event and its date.

## Series guides without What to watch
Guide-only series (HSR, Supercup, Moto2, Moto3, GT2/GT4 Europe, McLaren Trophy Europe, GT3 Revival) carry `noWatch:true` in `data/series.js`: `tools/due_updates.js` ignores them, so the routines never write previews or results for them (saves usage). Porsche Carrera Cup Brazil is deliberately NOT matched by the `carrera` (North America) guide.

## Event schedules (`sessions` in `data/<track>.js`)
**Full schedule rule (Nov 2 2026): this is an at-track app, so every weekend lists the on-track sessions of EVERY series that races there, including series the app does not track (e.g. F4 Brazilian Championship, Porsche Carrera Cup Brazil at the São Paulo GP); name them "<Session> - <Series>" and add the series to the event's `support` list. Use the organiser's official timetable (e.g. formula1.com's full-timetable article).** Fans only need **practice, qualifying and races** (plus the odd headline on-track event like a pole shootout or
parade). Leave out team-only test/shakedown days, paddock logistics, briefings, registration, tech inspection,
paid test sessions, autograph sessions. The official schedule PDFs list all of it -- filter it down.

**Schedule check, 3 weeks out** (Oct 28 2026): `node tools/due_schedules.js [--now ISO] [--ahead 21] [--text]` lists every weekend of a main series that starts within 21 days and has no session in its track file matching the series (`support` = support series of that event also missing; `late` from 3 days before). The weekly cloud routine "Trackside Pass: Schedule check (3 weeks out)" (trig_01AhPgtgbqU99kPaXb2uNFK8, Tuesday 03:00 UTC = Monday evening in New York, changed from daily on Nov 2 2026, no connectors) runs it, stops at once when nothing is due, otherwise finds the published timetable (series site, then the track's weekend page, then two agreeing media sources; never guess), writes the `sessions`, validates and pushes; a weekend still empty 3 days out is reported as late in the run's answer. The NASCAR support series (O'Reilly `oreilly`, Craftsman Trucks `trucks`) are opt-in support series; their session names deliberately omit the word NASCAR so the Cup guide does not attach to them. Martinsville and Homestead-Miami 2026 were written by hand from the tracks' own weekend-schedule pages (homesteadmiamispeedway.com/nascar-championship-weekend-schedule, martinsvillespeedway.com/weekend-schedule: both open in the built-in browser, not through WebFetch; the page's `.weekendschedule-content-item` rows carry every session).

## "What to watch" data (`data/watch.js`)
One entry per series per weekend: `{t: track id, d: event start day, s: series id (see SERIES in data/series.js), from?: first day it shows, asOf, groups:[{h: class, items:[[car or driver, why]]}], note: sources}`. The app shows only the NEWEST entry per series whose `from` has arrived (`watchNow()`: an entry with no `from` counts as the earliest, so ANY entry with a `from` replaces the weekend's first preview, and among equal `from` the later one in the array wins; until Oct 26 2026 an entry with no `from` was ranked by the event day, which made the Monday refresh of the Mexico City preview invisible until race day), so a mid-weekend update is a NEW entry with the same t/d/s and a later `from` that fully replaces the old one (write it as a fresh preview, drop stale claims, never an addendum). Old entries stay as history. The Race Day hub shows the entry for the series currently on track; overnight (nothing live, and the next session is on a later day than the last one, or the weekend has not started) it shows the headlining series' (`headlineSeries()`: the first main series you follow racing that weekend) latest entry until `HUB_SWITCH_LEAD_MIN` (30) minutes before the day's first session, then follows whichever series is up next (a starred series still wins; Standings follow the same series).

## F1 sprint weekends (Nov 6 2026)
Session names: "Sprint Qualifying - Formula 1" (qualifying), "Sprint - Formula 1" (a race), "Qualifying - Formula 1", "Race - Formula 1" (the Grand Prix). `tools/f1_update.py` writes TWO Pick-your-podium entries on a sprint weekend, like IndyCar's Milwaukee doubleheader: the Sprint game (key d = weekend start, `label:"Sprint"`, `lockRace:"^Sprint - Formula 1"`, form-only odds because the sprint grid is not in Jolpica until after the Sprint) and the Race game (key d = the weekend's last day, `label:"Race"`, locks at the Grand Prix). `gameNow()` shows the first game that is neither scored nor locked. Results: the Sprint result is keyed by the weekend start day (event "<race> (Sprint)"), the Grand Prix by the last day; `hubResultsCard` picks the key. `/api/session?s=f1&date=<Sprint day>` returns the Sprint classification (Sprint mode in `netlify/functions/session.mjs`). No sprint-qualifying results exist in Jolpica, so after Sprint Qualifying the hub shows a "Sprint qualifying" card that links to the live timing; the "Qualifying order" card appears after the main Qualifying.

## Server-side caching (Nov 6 2026)
worker/index.js: /api/session and /api/gtwce are cached per Cloudflare data centre for 25 / 30 s (Cache API; x-cache: edge on a hit); /api/weather caches itself for 10 minutes; /api/ytlive goes through ONE Durable Object per channel (class YtLiveCache, binding YT_LIVE and a migration in wrangler.jsonc): one shared answer for 60 s worldwide, one lookup shared by simultaneous callers, and the 100-unit YouTube search at most once every 10 minutes per channel, so many thousands of phones stay far inside the 10,000-unit daily YouTube quota. Without the binding the function runs directly. Tested locally with npx wrangler dev (12 simultaneous calls all answered, the repeat said x-cache: shared). If the Cloudflare deploy ever fails on the migration, remove durable_objects and migrations from wrangler.jsonc: the Worker falls back to a direct lookup.
